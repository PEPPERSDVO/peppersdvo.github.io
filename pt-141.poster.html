Bremelanotide (PT-141) 10mg

QUICK GUIDE

On-demand melanocortin for desire

What is it?

Bremelanotide (PT-141) activates melanocortin receptors in the brain involved in sexual desire. It's FDA-approved as Vyleesi, 1.75 mg as needed, for premenopausal women with low sexual desire. Nausea and a temporary blood-pressure rise are its main effects.

Strongest evidence: A · Approved use

Reconstitution

Add 2 mL bacteriostatic water to the 10 mg vial.

This yields 5 mg/mL.
  1. Swab both vial tops with an alcohol pad.
  2. Draw 2 mL of water and inject it slowly down the vial wall.
  3. Roll gently until dissolved; don't shake.
  4. Label and refrigerate.

Potential benefits

  • Central action: MC4 receptors in the brain, not blood flow. A · Approved use
  • Nausea: 40% overall; 21% after the first dose. A · Approved use
  • Blood pressure: Transient rise after each dose. A · Approved use
  • Pigmentation: Focal darkening in 1%. A · Approved use

Potential side effects

  • Nausea 40% (21% first dose, fades)
  • Flushing 20%
  • Transient blood-pressure rise
  • Focal hyperpigmentation 1%

The catalog's standard dose is 2–2.5 mg. The approved dose is 1.75 mg.

The catalog's monthly cap (8 doses) matches the label's frequency limit.

For subcutaneous injection
Refrigerate after mixing. Catalog: use within 60 days.
