What is Semaglutide?

Semaglutide is a once-weekly GLP-1 receptor agonist: the molecule in Wegovy and Ozempic. It reduces appetite and slows gastric emptying, and it improves blood-sugar control. Of the drugs on this list, it has the deepest evidence base, including a cardiovascular outcomes trial.

Strongest evidence: A · Approved use

Reconstitution

Add 1 mL bacteriostatic water to the 5 mg vial.

This yields 5 mg/mL.
  1. Swab both vial tops with an alcohol pad.
  2. Draw 1 mL of water and inject it slowly down the vial wall.
  3. Roll gently until dissolved; don't shake.
  4. Label and refrigerate.

Potential benefits

  • Weight loss: −14.9% at 2.4 mg over 68 weeks (STEP 1). A · Approved use
  • Cardiovascular: MACE −20% in established CVD (SELECT). A · Approved use
  • Glycemic control: Approved for type 2 diabetes (Ozempic). A · Approved use
  • High dose: 7.2 mg: ≈ −20.7% at 72 weeks (STEP UP). B · Human trials
  • Appetite: Central GLP-1R satiety + slower gastric emptying. A · Approved use
  • Regain: About two-thirds of the loss returns within a year of stopping. B · Human trials

Potential side effects

  • Nausea, diarrhea, vomiting and constipation are the most common (dose-related)
  • Mostly during dose escalation
  • Dysesthesia more frequent at 7.2 mg (22%)
  • Gallbladder events with rapid weight loss

Catalog: 'half lost 17%, a third lost 20%'. STEP 1 says 50.5% lost ≥15% and 32.0% lost ≥20%.

The catalog lists doses to 2.5 mg; approved maintenance doses are 2.4 mg (Wegovy), up to 2 mg (Ozempic) or 7.2 mg (Wegovy HD).
