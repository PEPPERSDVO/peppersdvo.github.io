What is Vitamin B12?

Vitamin B12 is needed to make red blood cells and maintain nerves. Injections are FDA-approved to treat deficiency, including pernicious anemia, at 100 mcg a month. The catalog's 5,000 mcg doses are 50 times that, and most of an excess dose is passed in urine.

Strongest evidence: A · Approved use

Preparation

Pre-made solution: no mixing.

  1. Swab the vial top with an alcohol pad.
  2. Draw the dose straight from the vial.
  3. Refrigerate; follow the vial label.

Potential benefits

  • Approved: Cyanocobalamin injection for B12 deficiency. A · Approved use
  • Excretion: 50–98% of a 1,000 mcg dose ends up in urine. A · Approved use
  • Energy claim: No evidence of benefit without deficiency. N/E · Not enough evidence

Potential side effects

  • Injection-site pain
  • Rare hypersensitivity (cobalt)

Vial size isn't stated; 10 mg/mL is implied by the catalog's own unit math.

The catalog says not to use supplemental B12 with chronic kidney problems. That isn't a label contraindication (the label lists cobalt sensitivity).
