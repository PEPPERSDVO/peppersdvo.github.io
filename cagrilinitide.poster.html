What is Cagrilintide?

Cagrilintide is a once-weekly long-acting amylin analog. Amylin is a satiety hormone released with insulin, and it acts on a different pathway from GLP-1. Alone it produced about 11% weight loss in phase 2. Paired with semaglutide (CagriSema), it reached about 20% in phase 3.

Strongest evidence: B · Human trials

Reconstitution

Add 2 mL bacteriostatic water to the 10 mg vial.

This yields 5 mg/mL.
  1. Swab both vial tops with an alcohol pad.
  2. Draw 2 mL of water and inject it slowly down the vial wall.
  3. Roll gently until dissolved; don't shake.
  4. Label and refrigerate.

Potential benefits

  • Monotherapy: Up to −10.8% at 4.5 mg over 26 weeks (phase 2). B · Human trials
  • With semaglutide: −20.4% vs −3.0% at 68 weeks (REDEFINE 1). B · Human trials
  • Satiety: Amylin receptors in the area postrema and hypothalamus. B · Human trials
  • Fast catalog ladder: Catalog escalates weekly; trials took 6–16 weeks. E · Community / vendor

Potential side effects

  • Nausea: 20–47% vs 18% placebo (phase 2)
  • Other GI effects, dose-related
  • Injection-site reactions
  • Combined with semaglutide: more GI effects than either alone

Escalation: the catalog steps up every week to 2.7 mg in 7 weeks. REDEFINE 1 took 16 weeks to reach 2.4 mg, and phase 2 took up to 6 weeks.

Top dose: the catalog's 2.7 mg is above the 2.4 mg phase 3 dose.

Refrigerate after mixing. Catalog: use within 60 days.
